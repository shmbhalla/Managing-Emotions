Lesson 1 · Full 30-Minute Session

God Made Emotions 🤍

Feelings are not bad — and they're not "everything" either. They are a gift from God, part of being made in His image. In this lesson we'll explore three truths about feelings — each with a game!

"Out of the depths I cry to you, O LORD! O Lord, hear my voice!"Psalm 130:1–2 — our memory verse for this lesson

⏱ The 30-minute plan:

🎭
Warm-Up: "Feeling Faces" charades — 5 min
📖
Three truths about feelings, each with a mini-game — 14 min
🌈
Share & Create: "Feeling Rainbow" — 5 min
🎮
Consolidation game: "Emotion Match" — 5 min
🙏
"3-2-1" exit ticket + closing prayer — 3 min
Big picture: our culture has two extremes — people who say feelings are everything, and people who say feelings are nothing. The Bible says: feelings are a gift.
Gr. 3–4: Stick to the plan and let the games lead — they learn by moving and laughing.
Gr. 5–6 / JHS: Add 1–2 extra discussion minutes to each truth, and push the "where do YOU fit?" question between the two extremes.

Lesson 1 · Truth #1 · ⏱ 5 min

Emotions are From God 🤍

You can't read the creation story without feeling awe, grandeur, joy, love and wonder (Genesis 1) — even the angels were singing and shouting for joy as God finished creating (Job 38:7)! Because we're made in God's image, feeling is part of who we are.

"When he saw the crowds, he had compassion for them, because they were harassed and helpless, like sheep without a shepherd."Matthew 9:36

🔎 Mini-game: Which feeling did God / Jesus show? Tap it!

Gr. 3–4: Read each scene like a storybook before the kids tap.
Gr. 5–6 / JHS: After the game ask: "If God has feelings, what does that tell us about how He sees OUR feelings?" (He gets them — and made room for them in His family.)
Lesson 1 · Truth #2 · ⏱ 5 min

Not All Emotions Are Bad 💧

One of the most striking verses in the Bible is only two words long: "Jesus wept." (John 11:35) Jesus was perfect — and He knew He would raise Lazarus minutes later — yet He still cried. Negative feelings can be the right feelings. Time for Myth or Truth!

Gr. 3–4: Read each statement aloud; the class votes with thumbs or fingers, then someone taps.
Gr. 5–6 / JHS: After each reveal ask "Why do people believe this myth?" — usually the fear that bad feelings = bad faith. Point to Psalm 130:1 as the model of HONEST prayer.
Lesson 1 · Truth #3 · ⏱ 4 min

Feelings Show What You Love 💛

People watching Jesus cry said, "See how he loved him!" How did they know? Because feelings are like a flashlight — they reveal what matters most to our hearts. Only the people and things we truly love stir our strongest feelings.

🔮 Feeling Forecast: In each situation, tap the feeling you would feel most. (No wrong answers — be honest!)

Gr. 3–4: Keep it light and fun; celebrate every honest answer.
Gr. 5–6 / JHS: Close with the hard question from the material: "If our feelings never moved in worship... could we claim to love God? What would a heart that loves God actually FEEL like?"

Lesson 2

The Big Story of Feelings 📜

Feelings fit into the Bible's big story: before the Fall → fallen → redeemed → future. Tap each card to open it!

🌅 Before the Fall
In the Garden of Eden, Adam and Eve had perfect feelings. No fear, no shame, no loneliness — perfect shalom (peace and wholeness). They could be naked in God's presence without hiding. (Genesis 1:26–27)
💔 Fallen
Rebellion shattered everything. God called, "Where are you?" and Adam said, "I was afraid... so I hid." (Genesis 3:10) Sin broke our feelings — like dropping a glass: everyone is broken in different shapes and sizes (some in anger, some in loneliness).
✨ Redeemed
Jesus came "that they may have life, and have it abundantly" (John 10:10). Salvation means our whole self is being repaired: minds renewed (Romans 12:2), wills aligned, feelings realigned — and the Holy Spirit grows brand-new feelings in us: the fruit of the Spirit (Galatians 5:22). It's still in progress!
👑 Future
"When he appears we shall be like him" (1 John 3:2). In heaven: perfect love, endless joy, never-ending peace — no more anger, bitterness, or hatred. All feelings in full bloom! 🌸

We live in "Redeemed" today — that's why feelings sometimes still misbehave. God is fixing it, not finished with it yet.

Gr. 3–4: Use the broken-glass picture: "Everyone is a little broken, just in different shapes — and God is the best repairer." Great for showing we are all in this together.
Gr. 5–6 / JHS: Ask: "If your feelings are 'still being repaired,' what changes how you feel about your failures?" Discuss already/not-yet: saved now, perfected in heaven.
Lesson 3

Beyond Fear & Anxiety 🌊

🚤 The disciples got scared in a huge storm — even after seeing Jesus do miracles. Fear is normal. So what do we DO with it?

"And he awoke and rebuked the wind and said to the sea, 'Peace! Be still!'... He said to them, 'Why are you so afraid? Have you still no faith?'"Mark 4:39–40
🏖️
1. Cast your cares on God. "Casting" means throwing! God doesn't want you holding your fears — throw them to Him in prayer. Name each worry to God specifically, and His peace "will guard your hearts and minds" (Philippians 4:6–7; 1 Peter 5:6–7).
🙏
2. Confess sin & doubts. Sometimes fear is rooted in doubt about God's love. The Bible says: "Confess your sins to one another and pray for one another, that you may be healed" (James 5:16). You don't have to carry it alone!
🪨
3. Declare who God is. "The LORD is my rock and my fortress... my shield" (Psalm 18:2). When you can't control the storm, remind yourself: God is in control. "Be still, and know that I am God" (Psalm 46:10).
Gr. 3–4: Act it out: one kid is the stormy boat (wobbling), class shouts "PEACE, BE STILL!" — boat stands up straight and smiles.
Gr. 5–6 / JHS: Ask: "What's the difference between healthy fear (danger!) and anxious fear (worst-case scenarios)?" Connect "guard your heart" to what they feed on: news, social media, games — what's fueling the anxiety?

Lesson 4

No Bitter Root 🌱🚫

Bitterness is like a weed: it starts tiny ("it'll be fine...") but if nobody pulls it, it chokes the whole garden of your heart — and hurts other people too.

"See to it that no 'root of bitterness' springs up and causes trouble, and by it many become defiled."Hebrews 12:15
💧
1. Soak in God's grace every day. When your heart is filled with God's kindness and forgiveness for YOU, there's no room left for bitterness. Grace in → bitterness out. (Ephesians 4:32)
🔍
2. Spot the weed early. Unforgiveness, resentment, "I will NEVER talk to them again"... those are weeds sprouting! Tell God about them quickly — and get help from people you trust. God can help you "control your emotions and not allow your emotions to control you."
🕊️
3. Forgive — because He forgave you. You can't manufacture forgiveness on your own. But look at the cross: God forgave you first, completely. "We can only give what we have received." (Ephesians 4:32)

⚠️ Forgiveness ≠ friendship forever. Sometimes forgiveness means releasing the anger while still keeping safe boundaries. Ask a trusted adult or pastor for help.

Gr. 3–4: Story time: "Someone took your pen AND broke it AND laughed." Ask what a bitter heart would do vs. a forgiven heart. Use a small potted plant as the "bitter root" prop.
Gr. 5–6 / JHS: Group discussion: why do people hold onto grudges? Introduce the difference between forgiveness (releasing a debt) and reconciliation (restoring a relationship) — one always can happen, the other takes time and safety.

Lesson 5

When Things Go Wrong 😕

Lost the game. Missed the cut. Rained on picnic day. Disappointment is a fact of life in a broken world — but it is not the final word.

"I will bless the LORD at all times; his praise shall continually be in my mouth... Magnify the LORD with me, and let us exalt his name together!"Psalm 34:1–3
📸
1. Make God BIGGER than the disappointment. David had every reason to complain — but he chose to praise at all times. He magnified God, not his problems. Focus on who we HAVE (Christ) instead of what we DON'T have. Try to fix everything by yourself and you'll be disappointed again and again. 😅
👫
2. Don't face it alone. Notice David's invitation: "Magnify the LORD with me... together!" Disappointments are heavier alone. You need a team — family, church, friends — that reminds you who God is and points you back to Him.
Gr. 3–4: "What's one thing that didn't go your plan this week?" Have them share, then the class offers one prayer word each.
Gr. 5–6 / JHS: Deeper question: "Is wanting to control things the same as wanting to prepare? Where's the line?" Discuss surrender vs. laziness — trust doesn't mean we stop trying.

Lesson 6

Not Alone 💜

Everyone has felt lonely — new school, moving, being left out, losing someone. Here's the good news: God is not far away. He is near.

"Turn to me and be gracious to me, for I am lonely and afflicted... The friendship of the LORD is for those who fear him."Psalm 25:14–16
🤝
1. Call out to God — He's near. God is "Father of the fatherless and protector of widows" (Psalm 68:5). Lonely moments can become private moments with God — the One who can truly satisfy your heart.
🚁
2. Ask God to rescue you. "My soul is deeply troubled... bring me out of my distress" (Psalm 25:17). Feeling stuck and helpless? God promises deliverance for those who come to Him. There IS a way out of the loneliness pit.
🏠
3. Connect with your community. "God sets the lonely in families" (Psalm 68:6). God also uses people to comfort us. That's why church is a family, not a club (Ephesians 2:19). Open up to someone. Let them be there for you.
Gr. 3–4: Read Psalm 25:16 out loud. "The Bible writer felt EXACTLY the same thing. God still loves you."
Gr. 5–6 / JHS: Important distinction: solitude (good, on purpose, with God) vs. isolation (painful, pushing people out). Ask: "Is scrolling all night connecting you or isolating you?" Gently screen for deeper loneliness — refer to counselor if needed.

Lesson 7

Learning to Rest in God 😴

Between school, chores, activities, and homework... we can get burnt out. Jesus said: tap the cards to learn the secret word — R.E.S.T.

Rtap me!
😇Real rest is found only in God.
"Come to me, all who labor and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest." (Matthew 11:28)
Vacations and screen-time can distract — but only God rests your soul.
Etap me!
🔍Evaluate your life & heart.
"He gives to his beloved sleep" (Psalm 127:2).
Are you going too fast? Are you chasing the wrong goals? Check your heart.
Stap me!
🕯️Set apart quiet time with God.
Remember the Sabbath (Exodus 20:8).
God rested right after creating everything! Turn the noise off, be still, let God speak.
Ttap me!
👥Talk to people who lift you up.
"Two are better than one... if they fall, one will lift up his fellow" (Ecclesiastes 4:9–10).
Solitude is good. Isolation is not. Keep good people close!
Gr. 3–4: After flipping, do a "body rest": 3 slow deep breaths together while reading Matthew 11:28.
Gr. 5–6 / JHS: Ask: "Where have you tried to find rest that didn't actually rest you?" Discuss screen burnout and the difference between distraction and rest.
Lesson 8

Grow an Emotionally Healthy Life 🌻

Jesus compared our lives to a garden (Matthew 13). Healthy plants need more than pulling weeds — they need cultivation: water, care, sunshine. So do we!

📖
1. Read your Bible & pray daily. Your feelings follow your thoughts. Feed your mind with God's Word and God guards your heart (Philippians 4:7; Romans 12:2). You can't grow healthy if you never plant!
🌳
2. Go outside. "The heavens declare the glory of God" (Psalm 19:1). Nature preaches about God all day long — and it calms anxious hearts. Bigger sky = bigger view of God = smaller problems.
🎵
3. Sing in worship. God sings (Zephaniah 3:17), God told us to sing (Psalm 33:3), and we'll sing forever (Revelation 5). Singing moves our feelings toward God — even (especially!) when you're out of tune. 🎤
⛪
4. Stay connected to community. "Not neglecting to meet together... but encouraging one another" (Hebrews 10:25). Emotional health doesn't grow in a bubble — it grows with other people.
🎯
5. Live for God's purpose. You were made in God's image (Genesis 1:26–27) — made to honor Him and show Him to the world. A life with purpose is a soul at rest.
Gr. 3–4: Give each kid a paper seed to "plant" — one habit they'll try this week.
Gr. 5–6 / JHS: Discuss 1 Timothy 6:11 — we don't only flee bad feelings, we actively pursue good ones. Ask: "Which of the 5 habits do you already do? Which one do you need to start?"
